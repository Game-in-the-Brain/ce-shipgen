# Mneme Space Combat — Ship Design, Crew, and Superiority

> **Source:** Mneme Variant Space Combat Rules v2.45

---

## 1. Ship Design for Combat

### Crew Requirements

A ship needs sufficient crew to operate its systems. Each system requires crew to function:

| System | Crew Needed |
|--------|-------------|
| Bridge | 1 per station |
| Engineering | 1 per power plant + 1 per M-drive |
| Gunnery | 1 per turret, 3 per bay |
| Sensors | 1 per sensor system |
| Maintenance | 1 per 100 DT of ship (rounded down) |
| Command | 1 (Captain or Officer) |

### Bridge Stations

The bridge requires stations for:
- **Captain** — Leadership, tactics, coordination
- **Pilot** — Maneuvering, evasion
- **NavComm** (Navigation and Communication) — Sensors, navigation, comms
- **Gunner** (or separate gunnery stations)
- **Engineer** (or separate engineering station)

### Automation

Ships can have automation to reduce crew requirements:
- **Automation Level 1:** Halves crew requirements (minimum 1)
- **Automation Level 2:** Quarter crew requirements (minimum 1)
- Higher automation = higher cost and potential vulnerabilities

### Ship Architecture

Ships can be designed with different architectures affecting combat:

| Architecture | Effect |
|--------------|--------|
| Standard | Normal hit location distribution |
| Streamlined | +1 thrust effective for maneuvering |
| Dispersed | +20% HP distributed across sections (harder to destroy) |
| Planetoid | +50% armor HP, -1 thrust |
| Buffered Planetoid | +100% armor HP, -2 thrust |

## 2. Crew Roles in Combat

### Phase Order

Combat proceeds in phases by crew role:

1. **Captain** — Issues orders, tactics, leadership
2. **NavComm** — Sensors, navigation, communication, electronic warfare
3. **Engineer** — Power management, damage control, repairs
4. **Pilot** — Maneuvering, evasion, positioning
5. **Gunner** — Weapons fire, missile control
6. **Others** — Any remaining crew actions

### Captain

- **Leadership** check to give orders
- Can use **Tactics** to gain initiative or positioning advantage
- Can coordinate crew for bonuses
- **Reaction:** Countermand, Override

### NavComm (Navigation and Communication)

- **Sensors** check to detect enemies, identify ships, assess damage
- **Navigation** to plot courses, predict enemy movement
- **Comms** to jam, hack, or communicate
- **Reaction:** Electronic Countermeasures (ECM)

### Engineer

- **Engineering** check to manage power
- Can overdrive systems for temporary bonuses (risk of failure)
- **Repairs** to fix damaged systems
- **Reaction:** Damage Control, Emergency Power

### Pilot

- **Piloting** check for maneuvering
- Evasive maneuvers (increase defense)
- Positioning (close/open range)
- **Reaction:** Evasive Action, Emergency Thrust

### Gunner

- **Gunnery** check to fire weapons
- Can choose targets (called shots)
- Manage ammunition and heat
- **Reaction:** Point Defense, Fire Discipline

## 3. Superiority

### Superiority Calculation

**Count** the following on each side:
- **Stations** (bridge stations, command centers)
- **Turrets** (each turret counts as 1)
- **Bays** (each bay counts as 1)
- **Sensors** (each sensor system counts as 1)
- **Fighters/Small Craft** in the encounter

**Do NOT use tonnage** for superiority in Mneme Space Combat.

Compare the totals:
- Side with more = superior
- Difference in count = DM modifier to the superior side's actions

| Superiority Difference | DM |
|------------------------|-----|
| 1-2 | +1 |
| 3-5 | +2 |
| 6-10 | +3 |
| 11-20 | +4 |
| 21+ | +5 |

### Group Actions and Superiority

When multiple ships fight together, calculate superiority as a group.

### Multiple Ship Combat

- Escorts can protect larger ships
- Fighters can swarm larger ships
- Superiority shifts dynamically as ships are destroyed or join

## 4. NPC Ship Handling

### Simplifying NPC Ships

For the Referee, simplify NPC ships:
- Group similar ships together
- Use average stats for ship groups
- Focus on 2-3 significant NPCs, abstract the rest with MAC

### NPC Crew Quality

| Quality | Skill Level | Description |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Green | 8-9 | Inexperienced, +1 DM to opponent |
| Regular | 10-11 | Average trained crew |
| Veteran | 12-13 | Experienced, +1 DM to their actions |
| Elite | 14+ | Best of the best, +2 DM to their actions |

### Ship Tiers and AI

The AI Pilot module uses these tiers for decision-making:

| Tier | Aggression | Flee Threshold | Notes |
|------|-----------|----------------|-------|
| Pirate | 80% | 20% hull | Aggressive, attacks weaker prey |
| Raider | 90% | 10% hull | Very aggressive, attacks then flees |
| Patrol | 50% | 40% hull | Defensive, protects territory |
| Merchant | 20% | 50% hull | Avoids combat, flees when threatened |
| Military | 70% | 30% hull | Disciplined, follows orders |

## 5. Damage Control and Repairs

### Emergency Repairs (Engineer Action)

- Takes **1D6×10 minutes** per attempt
- **Engineering** check: Success restores 1D6 damage to a section
- Critical failure causes additional 1D6 damage

### Dock Repairs

- At a port or base
- Full repairs take **1 day per 10 HP of damage**
- Cost: **0.1 MCr per HP** (or as per ship maintenance rules)

### Jury Rigging

- Temporary repairs that last **1 encounter**
- **Engineering** check at -2
- Restores **half** the normal repair amount
- May fail at critical moments (GM discretion)

## 6. Life Support and Hull Breaches

### Hull Breach

When Hull is depleted:
- Ship loses atmosphere
- Crew must wear vacc suits
- Operations continue but at reduced efficiency

### Life Support Damage

When Life Support is hit:
- Gradual loss of atmosphere
- Temperature regulation fails
- Crew takes fatigue and eventually damage

---

## 7. Radiation Damage

Weapons with **rad hits** cause radiation exposure:
- Crew in exposed sections take radiation damage
- Vacc suits and ship shielding provide protection
- Radiation sickness takes effect after the encounter

---

## 8. Special Combat Situations

### Boarding Actions

- Ships must be **Adjacent** range
- Boarding party uses personal combat rules
- Defenders have cover advantage

### Ramming

- Ships must be **Adjacent** or **Close**
- Both ships take damage based on relative velocity and mass
- Damage = (Thrust difference + Mass difference) × 1D6

### Minefields

- Ships entering a minefield roll for detection
- Undetected mines attack automatically
- Detected mines can be avoided or cleared

## 9. Design Trade-offs

### Armor vs. Speed

| Approach | Benefit | Drawback |
|----------|---------|----------|
| Heavy Armor | Survives more hits | Less thrust, slower |
| Fast Ship | Better initiative, can dictate range | Less armor, fragile |
| Balanced | Moderate in all | No standout advantage |

### Weapon Loadouts

| Loadout | Best Against | Weakness |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| Missile Heavy | Slow, armored ships | Point defense, ammo limited |
| Beam Heavy | Fast, light ships | Armor, shorter range |
| Mixed | Flexible | Less maximum damage |
| Fighter Carrier | Overwhelms point defense | Carriers are vulnerable |

## 10. Quick Reference: Combat Sequence

1. **Setup:** Determine encounter type, starting range, superiority
2. **Initiative:** 2D6 + thrust/leadership modifiers
3. **Phase 1 — Captain:** Leadership, tactics, orders
4. **Phase 2 — NavComm:** Sensors, navigation, ECM
5. **Phase 3 — Engineer:** Power, repairs, overdrive
6. **Phase 4 — Pilot:** Maneuver, evade, position
7. **Phase 5 — Gunner:** Fire weapons, missiles
8. **Phase 6 — Others:** Any remaining actions
9. **Reactions:** Resolve reactions as triggered
10. **Damage:** Apply damage at end of round
11. **Status:** Check for destroyed sections, crew casualties
12. **Next Round:** Repeat until one side flees, surrenders, or is destroyed
