CORE TASK RESOLUTION CHANGES
Core task resolutions in CESRD12 will have some minor changes. These changes will speed things up, like the Opposed
Checks, and will clarify some mechanics, as well as make some outcomes a bit more unpredictable.
Target Number (TN)
Die Rolls in CESRD page 14, the target number typically is 8+, but this can vary example: character creation CESRD34 for
Qualification, Survival, Commission, Advancement, and re-Enlistment and throughout the book (Examples are some skill
checks), there are different TN. It helps for the Character’s modifiers to be separated, and the circumstance DM and the
task affect the TN instead. It helps also when the Refereecan omit the TN if they cannot let the PC know if they succeed
or fail.
One Roll Opposed Checks Mechanics
In Mneme Space Combat, opposed rolls are streamlined to speed up gameplay. Unlike in CESRD page 15 where both
parties roll, here only the players roll. The Referee doesn't need to roll for NPCs. Players roll against a Target Number
(TN), which is typically 8 or higher, plus any Difficulty Modifiers (DM) that the adversary might have.
How It Works
Players roll a 2d6 and add their relevant skill modifier against a TN that is calculated as follows:
TN= 8(Base TN) + Adversary’s DM
For example, if an opponent has a Gunner DM of +2, the player would roll against a TN of 10 (8+2).
Types of Opposed Rolls
● Point Defense Roll: Players roll against a TN calculated as 8 + the Gunner's DM of the attacker.
● Dodge Roll: Players roll against a TN calculated as 8 + the Gunner's DM of the attacker.
● Electronic Warfare (EW) and Break Lock Check: Players roll against a TN calculated as 8 + the adversary’s
Comms-Sensor or Gunner DM.
● Range Check: Players roll an opposed Navigation Check against a TN calculated as 8 + the adversary’s Navigation DM.
Reactions and Difficulty Modifiers
● Reactions that Increase Difficulty: If players take certain reactions, they receive a bonus to their opposed roll result.
Conversely, if the enemy takes a reaction, it increases the TN for the roll.
● Minor Actions or Reactions: Parties wishing to oppose a roll must use up a Minor Action or Reaction to do so. If they
choose not to oppose, the difficulty is reduced by 2. For example, Missile Attack rolls without any Point Defense or
Pilot Dodging would be against a TN of 6+ instead of 8+.
Speeding Up Play: Players roll for Adversaries
Players have the option to roll for their adversaries and NPCs, effectively reducing the number of rolls the Referee has to
make during space combat to zero. However, Referees still have the option to roll for NPCs if they choose to.
Minimum Success Effect of 1
See Difficulty and Effect CESRD page 15 which puts success at an effect of 0, a Success should have at least an effect of 1.
Degree of Success equals the difference of check results minus the target number (typically 8) plus 1.
● Example: A 2d6 and DM+1 Roll that rolls a 7 plus DM+1 = 8, against a Target number of 8: 8 minus 8 = 0 + 1. So the
effect is at least 1.
● this is a rules clarification:
○ Effects determine additional Initiative, a Successful Tactics Roll doesn't grant any Effect and thus no Initiative
Advantage.
○ Effects determine additional DM+1s a successful Coordinate Crew action will grant, an effect 0 will grant none.
○ This will add to all Successful Attacks to have a damage of 1.
Double Effect Rolling 2 and 12
Upon rolling a 12 on the Dice (6 on each die, naturally, before any modifications are applied), double the effect of the roll
if it is positive (good) and halve it if it is negative (bad). Likewise, upon rolling a 2 (1 on each die, naturally), double the
effect of the roll if it is negative and halve it if it is positive.
For Example, a 2d6 DM+1 roll of 12 against a TN 8 has a total check result of 13 minus 8 for 5 effects (not enough for an
Exceptional Success despite a roll of 12). Rolling a 12 makes the 5 a 10 effect. Rolling a 2 with a 2D6 DM+1 against a TN of
8 equals a 3 minus 8 for a -5 (not an exceptional failure), doubling for a -10 effect.
● With Opposed Check mechanics, apply the modification to the Effect before doubling.
● Weapon Damage which adds the roll’s Effect is affected by this rule.
● With these rules, Critical Hits and Misses will have a higher effect on the game. These rules increase the odds of
having an effect of 6 or -6 as compared to the regular rules. Currently because of the TN 8, in a D20 system where
the extremes are rolled 5% of the time, in 2D6 these are rolled at 2.77% (either 2 or 12).
Superiority
This rule set is for situations where multiple people/characters are working on the same task and where it would be
inconvenient or absurd to roll for each of them separately or to add up the results directly. With this, it is assumed that,
for many tasks, whether or not these involve spaceships, the number of people performing the tasks does not add to the
effectiveness of those tasks in a linear manner and it takes more and more people to effectively help at the exact same
action. The rules are as follows:
Any other additional parties beyond the main characters aiding in a roll can be considered as adding to “Superiority”.
● A Superiority dice modifier is added as a bonus to the PC’s roll result if they have superior numbers and resources,
compared to the target or opposition to the action.
○ These resources are Stations and Manpower counted against the other party. Example: A ship with 2 Sensors
with 1 NavComm officer against a Squadron with 4 Sensors and 2 Officers, the squadron will have superiority: 2 vs
1. a DM+2 or Raising the TN by 2.
● A Superiority dice modifier is a penalty to the PC’s roll if the target or opposition has superior numbers and resources
compared to the target or opposition to the action.
This means that if all other circumstances are equal, the side that has the superior significant resources has an
advantage, measured in DM. Typically the Refereeconsiders ships and their resources: Ships, Turrets/Bays, Multiple
Electronic Suites and Stations as adding to Superiority.

This applies as a ratio, meaning that 4 vs 2 is as good as 2 vs 1 and 5 vs 3 is roughly equivalent to 1 ½ vs 1.
At the Referee’s discretion, certain qualitative advantages can be considered to make one side count for more than their
numbers, just as a machine gun may count for much more than a pistol and an armed warship more than a cargo ship
with guns. Despite the adversarial nature of Superiority rules, it’s best to use these for rolls unrelated to Attacking or
Defense, using multiple Attack rules instead for those.
●    In CE Space combat Hard Points (per 100DT) is one of the key constraints of the mechanics as designed that keep
ships from having ridiculous amounts of weaponry on them. Bay and Turret Weapons are constrained by Hardpoints.
○ Example: So a 200DT ship with 2 hard points is roughly equal to two Small Craft. Of course the quality of the small
craft can change this, as 2 Fighters 10-50DT will not take as much damage as two 95DT armed shuttles.
Do not Limit Superiority to the Number of People or Forces or Ships. Consider also Time to prepare as a metric of
Superiority. Two forces being equal, but one party had 3 times more time to prepare than the other party and change
the circumstance can be counted. You can also consider the Asymmetric Objectives as well - if One side had to put in 3x
the amount of work, than the other Party to win then the party that needs less work would be superior.
TL Superiority
If two Sensor or Electronics Systems are being used against each, the Difference in their TL is a DM modifier. Apply this
modifier to only one of the parties. Since the RefereeFocuses on the Players, the DM applies a TL Superiority Penalty to
the Players if the Adversary is of a Higher TL or a Bonus if the Players have the superior TL.
Multiple Actions Clarified (CESRD48)
Multiple Actions occur when a Character uses more than one Action in a Round or Turn. This means the character
Performs a Minor Action (For example, positioning the ship as a pilot) and a Significant Action (For example, an Attack).
Consider applying a penalty when different Skills are used for Multiple actions. Characters cannot perform more actions
than their 3 Minor Actions or 1 significant action and Minor action.
