MNEME VARIANT SPACE COMBAT
A game about space travel will almost inevitably require rules for space combat. The rules presented here are meant to
provide a relatively simple means of resolving space combat that doesn’t depart too strongly from the Traveller Combat
system.
● Use of a Map. Movement and maneuvering are abstracted to a 2D plane without measured distances to allow for
    cinematic conflict with tactical positioning with ranges simplified to Short, Medium, Long, and Very Long while
    combat itself is given a set of steps that minimize the number of combatants that need tracking and rolls that need
    to be made while still allowing for complex encounters.
● There is an Order of Turns set by Initiative and Function. Mneme space combat uses Phases, based on function. All
    sides can act during the phase where the order is based on their initiative, before the characters who are part of a
    later phase.
● Grouping Rolls. To minimize rolls and number tracking, the Referee can group ships they control if these ships are
    expected to act as one. The Referee can have rolls groups, instead of rolling for more than the number of players per
    game.
● Much Simpler Damage. in Mneme (1) we roll damage minus armor, (2) Roll Hit-Location Table, (3) Determine the
    conditions of that amount of damage to the Hit-Location's Hitpoints by checking the Damage Section Table. In CE
    RAW (1) We roll Damage, subtracting armor , (2) comparing the remaining difference to a damage table to determine
    the number of "Hits" (p. 159), (3) rolling the Hit Location table (p159), and (4) then Comparing to the Hit Location
    Effect (p.159 to 161) . 1 less step and table.
● More Examples. We have more examples for clarification, See https://youtu.be/9836WBRQKXE . We will give more
    examples that can act as scenarios to help prepare these Space Combat Encounters.


CORE TASK RESOLUTION CHANGES
Core task resolutions in CESRD12 will have some minor changes. These changes will speed things up, like the Opposed
Checks, and will clarify some mechanics, as well as make some outcomes a bit more unpredictable.

Target Number (TN)
Die Rolls in CESRD page 14, the target number typically is 8+, but this can vary example: character creation CESRD34 for
Qualification, Survival, Commission, Advancement, and re-Enlistment and throughout the book (Examples are some skill
checks), there are different TN. It helps for the Character’s modifiers to be separated, and the circumstance DM and the
task affect the TN instead. It helps also when the Refereecan omit the TN if they cannot let the PC know if they succeed
or fail.

One Roll Opposed Checks Mechanics
In Mneme Space Combat, opposed rolls are streamlined to speed up gameplay. Unlike in CESRD page 15 where both
parties roll, here only the players roll. The Referee doesn't need to roll for NPCs. Players roll against a Target Number
(TN), which is typically 8 or higher, plus any Difficulty Modifiers (DM) that the adversary might have.
How It Works
Players roll a 2d6 and add their relevant skill modifier against a TN that is calculated as follows:
                                               TN= 8(Base TN) + Adversary’s DM
For example, if an opponent has a Gunner DM of +2, the player would roll against a TN of 10 (8+2).
Types of Opposed Rolls
● Point Defense Roll: Players roll against a TN calculated as 8 + the Gunner's DM of the attacker.
● Dodge Roll: Players roll against a TN calculated as 8 + the Gunner's DM of the attacker.
● Electronic Warfare (EW) and Break Lock Check: Players roll against a TN calculated as 8 + the adversary’s
    Comms-Sensor or Gunner DM.
● Range Check: Players roll an opposed Navigation Check against a TN calculated as 8 + the adversary’s Navigation DM.
Reactions and Difficulty Modifiers
● Reactions that Increase Difficulty: If players take certain reactions, they receive a bonus to their opposed roll result.
    Conversely, if the enemy takes a reaction, it increases the TN for the roll.
● Minor Actions or Reactions: Parties wishing to oppose a roll must use up a Minor Action or Reaction to do so. If they
    choose not to oppose, the difficulty is reduced by 2. For example, Missile Attack rolls without any Point Defense or
    Pilot Dodging would be against a TN of 6+ instead of 8+.
Speeding Up Play: Players roll for Adversaries
Players have the option to roll for their adversaries and NPCs, effectively reducing the number of rolls the Referee has to
make during space combat to zero. However, Referees still have the option to roll for NPCs if they choose to.

Minimum Success Effect of 1
See Difficulty and Effect CESRD page 15 which puts success at an effect of 0, a Success should have at least an effect of 1.
Degree of Success equals the difference of check results minus the target number (typically 8) plus 1.
● Example: A 2d6 and DM+1 Roll that rolls a 7 plus DM+1 = 8, against a Target number of 8: 8 minus 8 = 0 + 1. So the
    effect is at least 1.
● this is a rules clarification:
   ○ Effects determine additional Initiative, a Successful Tactics Roll doesn't grant any Effect and thus no Initiative
       Advantage.
   ○ Effects determine additional DM+1s a successful Coordinate Crew action will grant, an effect 0 will grant none.
   ○ This will add to all Successful Attacks to have a damage of 1.

Double Effect Rolling 2 and 12
Upon rolling a 12 on the Dice (6 on each die, naturally, before any modifications are applied), double the effect of the roll
if it is positive (good) and halve it if it is negative (bad). Likewise, upon rolling a 2 (1 on each die, naturally), double the
effect of the roll if it is negative and halve it if it is positive.
For Example, a 2d6 DM+1 roll of 12 against a TN 8 has a total check result of 13 minus 8 for 5 effects (not enough for an
Exceptional Success despite a roll of 12). Rolling a 12 makes the 5 a 10 effect. Rolling a 2 with a 2D6 DM+1 against a TN of
8 equals a 3 minus 8 for a -5 (not an exceptional failure), doubling for a -10 effect.
● With Opposed Check mechanics, apply the modification to the Effect before doubling.
● Weapon Damage which adds the roll’s Effect is affected by this rule.
● With these rules, Critical Hits and Misses will have a higher effect on the game. These rules increase the odds of
      having an effect of 6 or -6 as compared to the regular rules. Currently because of the TN 8, in a D20 system where
      the extremes are rolled 5% of the time, in 2D6 these are rolled at 2.77% (either 2 or 12).

Superiority
This rule set is for situations where multiple people/characters are working on the same task and where it would be
inconvenient or absurd to roll for each of them separately or to add up the results directly. With this, it is assumed that,
for many tasks, whether or not these involve spaceships, the number of people performing the tasks does not add to the
effectiveness of those tasks in a linear manner and it takes more and more people to effectively help at the exact same
action. The rules are as follows:

Any other additional parties beyond the main characters aiding in a roll can be considered as adding to “Superiority”.
● A Superiority dice modifier is added as a bonus to the PC’s roll result if they have superior numbers and resources,
    compared to the target or opposition to the action.
   ○ These resources are Stations and Manpower counted against the other party. Example: A ship with 2 Sensors
       with 1 NavComm officer against a Squadron with 4 Sensors and 2 Officers, the squadron will have superiority: 2 vs
       1. a DM+2 or Raising the TN by 2.
● A Superiority dice modifier is a penalty to the PC’s roll if the target or opposition has superior numbers and resources
    compared to the target or opposition to the action.
This means that if all other circumstances are equal, the side that has the superior significant resources has an
advantage, measured in DM. Typically the Refereeconsiders ships and their resources: Ships, Turrets/Bays, Multiple
Electronic Suites and Stations as adding to Superiority.
● The table below is used to determine the size of the Superiority bonus or penalty.

Superiority Table
    Superiority       Difficulty Modifier         Examples
     1 ½+ vs 1               DM+1                 A 400DT corvette and 400DT merchant, against 800DT of Raiders
      2+ vs 1                DM+2                 A pair of 20DT fighters against a 100DT scout.
                                                  An Q-ship (400DT Merchant with pop-up Particle Beams), against a
      3+ vs 1                 DM+3
                                                  6 Armed FreeTrader 200DT Merchants.
      5+ vs 1                 DM+4                A carrier strike group against a lone carrier.
      7+ vs 1                 DM+5                A missile squadron, and their escorts, against a battered Raider.
     10+ vs 1                 DM+6                A full Battle Group against a single Destroyer.
This applies as a ratio, meaning that 4 vs 2 is as good as 2 vs 1 and 5 vs 3 is roughly equivalent to 1 ½ vs 1.
At the Referee’s discretion, certain qualitative advantages can be considered to make one side count for more than their
numbers, just as a machine gun may count for much more than a pistol and an armed warship more than a cargo ship
with guns. Despite the adversarial nature of Superiority rules, it’s best to use these for rolls unrelated to Attacking or
Defense, using multiple Attack rules instead for those.

●    In CE Space combat Hard Points (per 100DT) is one of the key constraints of the mechanics as designed that keep
     ships from having ridiculous amounts of weaponry on them. Bay and Turret Weapons are constrained by Hardpoints.
    ○ Example: So a 200DT ship with 2 hard points is roughly equal to two Small Craft. Of course the quality of the small
         craft can change this, as 2 Fighters 10-50DT will not take as much damage as two 95DT armed shuttles.

Do not Limit Superiority to the Number of People or Forces or Ships. Consider also Time to prepare as a metric of
Superiority. Two forces being equal, but one party had 3 times more time to prepare than the other party and change
the circumstance can be counted. You can also consider the Asymmetric Objectives as well - if One side had to put in 3x
the amount of work, than the other Party to win then the party that needs less work would be superior.

TL Superiority
If two Sensor or Electronics Systems are being used against each, the Difference in their TL is a DM modifier. Apply this
modifier to only one of the parties. Since the RefereeFocuses on the Players, the DM applies a TL Superiority Penalty to
the Players if the Adversary is of a Higher TL or a Bonus if the Players have the superior TL.

Multiple Actions Clarified (CESRD48)
Multiple Actions occur when a Character uses more than one Action in a Round or Turn. This means the character
Performs a Minor Action (For example, positioning the ship as a pilot) and a Significant Action (For example, an Attack).
Consider applying a penalty when different Skills are used for Multiple actions. Characters cannot perform more actions
than their 3 Minor Actions or 1 significant action and Minor action.

Attack Success Effect
On a Successful Attack, the Effect of the roll adds additional Damage.


MNEME SHIP DESIGN CHANGES FROM CE
Bridge Stations
Bridges can accommodate a number of Stations equal to its Bridge’s DTONS. Example: A Merchant Trader’s Bridge is
10DT and can have up to 10 crewmen work there. Typically the Stations are: Captain, NavComm, Engineering, Pilot, and
Gunners - typically 5. Bridge stations can change Function as a Minor Action, of course shifting to NavComm, Gunner, or
Pilot depends if there is such a function and equipment available. If there are no Fire Control systems remaining to
control, there is no function available for an additional Gunner.
Additional Sensors Stations.
Purchasing additional Sensors (CESRD127, Table: Ship Electronics) is constrained by the available number of Stations in
the Bridge. The number of Sensors determines the number of NavComm: Significant Action that use Sensors that are
allowed.
Trading Bridge Space for Ship Components.
At no Additional Cost Additional Bridge can be sacrificed for reasonable Ship Components (CE SRD page 128) like Briefing
Room, Locker, and Luxuries.
Life Pod (Mneme Space Combat Rule)
A Life pod is an escape pod, a fresher, a bed, and a place to perform Vacc Suit cleaning and maintenance. A typical 1 Dton
Lifepod can hold 3 Adults but can hold more children. 42 doses of Hibernation Drugs (confusingly named Fast in CE SRD
page 72) worth 8,400Cr. It has a Fuel Tank for 2 weeks of Active use, in low power mode the life pod can run systems 60x
its normal duration for a total of 2520 hibernating days (divided by 3 adults thats 840 days). 140,000 Credits plus the
8,400 Cr of Hibernation Drugs (42 doses).

Space Combat Encounter Setup
A few things must be determined even before the initiative is rolled for as follows:

1. Ambush-Quarry Encounters:
   In these scenarios, the ambusher tracks its quarry, who remains unaware of the pursuit. The ambusher aims to get
   within range and seize the initiative.
2. Chance Encounters:
    These occur when ships happen to be within sensor range of each other, creating a potential for intercept.

Ambush-Quarry Mechanics
The ambusher typically targets an unsuspecting ship that has an active transponder, is using active sensors, or is
otherwise predictable in its movements.

1. Step 1 Declare Ranges: The ambusher declares their intended approach range to the quarry. This sets the Difficulty
    Modifier (DM) and Target Number (TN) for the upcoming rolls.
2. Step 2 Opposed Detection: An opposed roll is made, pitting the ambusher's Navigation skill against the quarry's
    Comms (Sensor) skill. Note that the quarry has a +2 advantage over the ambusher.
    Ambush-Quarry Difficulty:
    For the ambusher, the base TN is 10+, modified by the quarry's sensors. For the quarry, the base TN is 6+, modified
    by the ambusher's actions.
3. Step 3 Outcome:
   a. If the ambusher succeeds and the quarry fails, the ambusher gains the effect of the roll as an initiative bonus and
        can initiate the encounter at the declared range.
   b. If the quarry succeeds and the ambusher fails, the quarry gains the effect of the roll as an initiative bonus.
        Additionally, the quarry detects the ambusher at a range greater than the ambusher's target range by a number
        of range bands equal to the effect level.
        Example. An Ambusher attempting to engage at Medium Range (2 spaces away), the Quarry succeeds by 2: The
        Quarry detects the Ambusher at Very Long (Long (Effect 1) and Very Long (Effect 2).
Chance Encounter Mechanics
Context:
This type of encounter assumes that both ships have their transponders turned off and that at least one of the ships has
enough thrust to eventually intercept the other. The default sensor roll uses Passive Sensors, as using Active Sensors
would act as a beacon, revealing the position of the ship using them.
1. Step 1 - Initial Detection Roll: Both parties make an Opposed Comms (Sensor) roll at a difficulty level of TN 10+, with
    the adversary's modifiers added to the TN.
2. Step 2- Outcome:
   a. Winning Party's Options: The party that wins the Opposed Comms (Sensor) roll detects the other party at a
        Distant range. They then have two options:
          i.   Initiate Ambush-Quarry Encounter: The winning party can choose to use the Ambush-Quarry Encounter
               mechanics to approach the other ship.
         ii.   Evade: The winning party can choose to evade the other ship. To do so, they make an Opposed Navigation
               roll at an Easy difficulty level (TN 6+), adding the other party's Comms (Sensor) modifiers and range
               modifiers to the TN.

Actions During the Encounter
Actions are the key element of the space combat system, they determine what the Players can do to change the outcome
of an encounter. First we discuss Turns, then Functions, and then the different kinds of Actions.

Action and Reaction Limits
There are limits to the number of Non-Player Characters that can take Functions (which determines the options with
regards to Actions) in Mneme Space Combat. The game mechanics are designed to provide the Referee options to
consolidate Functions so that the Referee rolls less and has a lower overhead.

Turns in a Space Combat
Each turn in space combat lasts around one kilosecond (1,000 seconds) of game time (or roughly 17 minutes). In a
combat turn, vessels have individual Initiative.
A character’s Function determines when they get to act during their ship’s turn, the sort of actions they can do, as well as
what they can react to. The Functions are as follows:

Leading a Function
In every function (e.g., Sensor, Navigation, Engineering, Gunner, Piloting), there is always a designated "lead character"
responsible for making the primary roll or action for that function. This approach is designed to streamline combat and
reduce the Referee's workload.

Standard Rule - Single Roll/Action: Typically, only one roll or action is made per function. Other Non-Player-characters in
the same function can assist the lead character, but they do not make separate rolls.

Exception - Player Characters: If there are multiple Player Characters in the same function, each may perform additional
actions, thus exceeding the standard one roll or action per function.

Squadron Rule - NPCs: In the case of a squadron of ships and fighters, only one roll is made for each function (Sensor,
Navigation, Engineering, Gunner, Piloting). NPCs in these functions serve to raise the stakes and difficulty but do not
require separate rolls, thus minimizing the Referee's management tasks.

Assistant Function.
● Assistant Functions are best used for NPCs that do not roll or get an Action. Assistant functions don't have any
   Actions or Reactions, instead they compliment a Major Function.
● Assistant Functions grant a Superiority bonus on rolls (or negate an adversary’s Superiority Bonus). If there is a
   NavComm officer with 3 NavComm Assistants (with sufficient stations) rolling against one NavComm adversary - the
   NavComm officer with Assistants may have a 4 vs 1 superiority against the lone NavComm adversary.
● If there are a lot of Player Characters, they can Take the Assistant Function, typically using the Aiding Another and the
   Referee allows them to use up the 3 Minor Actions and Reactions - if there are enough stations, fire-controls, and
   sensors.

Personal Actions and Boarding
Personal actions and Boarding occur at a time frame that is much shorter than what is used for ship combat. This means
that many personal actions may be done on a character’s turn, though some of these may distract from or penalize ship
encounter related rolls. It also means that the Ship Encounter stops or is paused when resolving something like the
boarding of a ship, though the referee can resolve boarding with some skill rolls if the players aren’t directly involved.

assisting without using a Reaction or Unused Minor Action if the major Function ever becomes abandoned.

MINOR ACTION (MA)
The most basic unit of Action in Space Combat is the Minor Action. Each turn has 3 Minor Actions (and 1 reaction for the
Non-Automated Major Functions).

SIGNIFICATION ACTION (SA)
A significant action costs Two Minor Actions. These are the most common actions that have a great impact in the game.

FOCUSED ACTION (FA)
This is a Type of action where all 3 Actions and Reactions are spent to ensure a better chance of success. Any Significant
Action can be turned into a FOCUSED action, but the trade-off is that no Reactions can be made and all 3 Minor Actions
are spent. This Grants a DM+2 to the Action. Can also be called Full Action.
After Taking a Focused Action - Characters cannot make any reactions.
Characters who made reactions in that turn cannot use Focused Actions.

REACTIONS (RA)
1. During a combat turn, a character can only make one reaction without any penalty.
2. Any additional reactions (either through Preparation or Sacrificing Initiative) that a character makes beyond the first
   in the same turn incur a cumulative penalty of DM-1 for each extra reaction. For instance, the second reaction would
   have a DM-1, the third a DM-2, and so on.
3. Opposing an Adversary’s Roll consumes a Reaction or Minor Action. Example: Range Check is an Opposed Roll, if the
   other party decides not to oppose they dont lose a Minor action or reaction, but the party performing the Range
   Check does it at a lower difficulty.

Tight Beam Communication
Ships typically communicate through a powerful laser communication system that allows it to communicate with targets
the entire length of the solar system. Game mechanics wise, 25 AU per TL. The Voyager probe is being communicated
through a Tight beam to earth over.
Ship Transponders can juggle between Tight and Broadcast, maintaining its Transponder signal in both Broadcast and
Tight Beam with ships it chooses to maintain. Typically a Ship has the Starport (or Downport’s) coordinates to make Tight
Beam transponder connection with it or can detect its faint broadcast and lock on to it. The Port will send it Traffic
information (Direction and Distance only) of other ships in the area that are considered without hostile intent.
Hailing another Ship, is requesting to have a Tight Beam connection where the ship identifies itself: Name, Captain, and
Purpose (where they are headed).
Broadcast (Communications) Stance
While tight beam communication is a targeted Laser, the Broadcast stance is general spherical signal emanating from the
ship. Because of the inverse-square law this is much weaker over a distance of AU equal to the TL of the Sensor times the
number of sensors broadcasting.
Example
Ship Transponders default to Broadcast Stance, and have it continuously on.
Near Planet Space.
A distance which is less than the radius of the nearest planet from that planet's surface. Large planets with hundreds of
thousands of KM diameter, like Jupiter (70,000 km radius), and Saturn (55,000 km radius) dont appear on the map, but
their direction is noted.
Going Dark.
When thrust, active sensors, transponders, and IFF are all turned off to have a smaller profile. With the thrust being
turned off, the ship is going ballistic (Traveling without acceleration). The ship’s engineer must roll an Engineering Check
DM-0/8+ to get rid of some heat with the fuel. The ship must dump fuel (with its heat) equal to 1d6 x 1% x thrust of the
ship. Fail Effect is the additional 1% of fuel dumped, and Success Effect is the %1 fuel saved.
Interplanetary Encounter
This is an encounter where it happens farther than the radius of the nearest planet. Example is an encounter beyond
6,000 km from Earth, 70,000 km from Jupiter, or 3,400 km from Mars
Near Planet Encounter.1
This is an encounter where it happens within the radius of the nearest planet. Example an encounter that happens within
the 1,200 km of Pluto, or within the 2,700km of Ganymede.
Thrust Points
Thrust points are an Abstraction of how movement works in a Space Encounter. A ship has an amount of TP equal to its
Thrust. This allows them to Move a space, a hex or square. A space is typically a hex equal to 1/12th the Very Long Range
of the Ship A Thrust Point is spent to move from adjacent space to Short, Close, and Adjacent (Ramming).
Point Defense
Shooting missiles or other projectiles as a form of defense.
Intercept
When two groups or parties are moving in a direction that will bring them within range of each other. This can be debris,
comets, asteroids, missiles, stealth ships, someone in a vac suit hurtling through space, a capital warship, or a Planet.
What they can do to each other depends on their capabilities
● Detect: Beginning with Sensors, are they able to get high-resolution information,
● Communication: Send tight beam communications
● Physically Affect them - within range of weapons or drones.

Note on Personal Actions
As a space combat turn represents over 150 personal combat rounds, it stands to reason that crew members may engage
in a lot of personal actions over the course of a single combat turn. Much of this is abstracted into the various space
combat actions. Most personal actions have minimal impact on space combat. For crew members that do want to pursue
a personal action during space combat, such as use a psionic ability, these actions count as minor actions on the space
combat scale. This glosses over the remainder of the character's time during the combat turn, and highlights the primary
acts that a character might want to pursue without taking away from the rest of the space combat.

       Note the Accuracy of the launch (CE SRD p.152) determines the TN the 2D6 roll for each missile if they hit.
    o When Do Missiles Hit in Cepheus Engine SRD RAW?
        According to page 157, missiles will hit their target after they have been in flight for either 1 or 2 rounds.
       Specifically, if a missile is launched on round 2, it can only hit its target on round 3. This means that missiles will
       strike their targets during the 2nd or 3rd round after their launch. For example, a ship could move from a "Long"
       range (10,000 km) to a "Very Long" range (25,000 km) within this time frame.
    o Range Clarification in Cepheus Engine SRD RAW
        The rules state that a missile can strike a target at "Distant" range. However, there is some ambiguity here.
       "Distant" range is defined on page 149 as being 50,000 km or greater. The problem arises when considering
       distances greater than 50,000 km, such as 200,000 km. Does this qualify as a "Distant" range for missile strikes? To
       resolve this, one could define "Distant" as a bracketed range of 50,000 km to 100,000 km and label anything
       beyond that as "Beyond Distant."

    the turn the missile can strike.
● Its an Opposed Roll in Mneme, in CE RAW the roll for point defense is separate and on the turn the missile can
    attack.

●   In Mneme you can launch a missile and attack on the same turn as long as you have the thrust points to move the

Planetary Maneuvers
Within close range of a planet, certain planetary maneuvers become possible.

Abstract Boarding Rules for Tabletop RPGs: Incorporating Damage and Risk
These rules are designed to manage abstract boarding actions. They are ideal for scenarios that do not directly involve
the player or when there's a need to expedite ship-to-ship conflicts without immersing into individual combat.
The objective of these rules is to determine which side sustains casualties during a boarding action until one side's
morale is compromised and they retreat or surrender.
Procedure for Abstract Boarding Actions:
    1. Prior to the initiation of a boarding action, the commanders and characters on each side decide and declare the
        level of risk they are willing to take in the upcoming combat. This decision should be backed by a clear
        explanation to the Refereeof what specifically they are doing that increases the risk. This risk level will impact the
        extent of casualties both sides could suffer. A PC can increase the risk by 1%.
    2. Once the risk level has been set, an Opposed Tactics Roll is made between the commanders of the attacking and
        defending sides. This roll signifies the strategic interplay taking place.
    3. Apply the modifiers to the Opposed Roll according to the specific circumstances of the encounter. Keep track of
        which side has a tactical advantage and apply the corresponding modifiers.
    4. The effects of the combat roll are as follows:
            ● The victorious side inflicts casualties on the losing side. Casualties are calculated as a base 5% loss, plus
                 an additional 5% for each point of Effect rolled by the winner.
            ● If the forces on each side are less than 100, convert the percentage of casualties into a damage pool
                 where each 1% equals a 1D6 dice. Characters with higher armor ratings draw more of the damage pool,
                 with each third point of Armor (rounded up) absorbing one 1D6.

36 of 75
              ● If both parties tie or the loser fails by only 1, both sides suffer a 5% casualty rate, representing the
                ferocity of the close combat.
    5. Despite emerging victorious, the winning side may still incur casualties. To ascertain this, they make another
        opposed roll. If they succeed, they avoid any loss. If they fail, they face losses calculated as 1% plus an additional
        1% for each point of fail Effect (modified by the amount of risk they took).
    6. Taking additional risk can increase the damage inflicted upon success by an additional 2% per PC or Heroic
        Commander, but also increases their potential casualties by 1% per PC or Heroic Commander upon winning.
        Should they lose the opposed tactics roll, their casualties increase by an additional 2% per level of the rolled
        effect.
The cycle of these steps repeats until the morale of one side breaks, effectively concluding the boarding action."\

Cepheus Engine Computers Quick Fix

Problem Statement
in CE SRD RAW The problem is that it's too easy to hack another system. A computer with a lower TL can hack into a
higher TL.
This becomes a problem as Superior computers become compromised by lesser computers of lower TL. Its a glaring
narrative problem. If the Referee had such a dilemma its not an easy fix, because computers are ubiquitous in the game
and a ruling on the fly can give the referee problems in the future.
Also there is a lack of clarity about how they can be used.
Solution: Mneme Computer Rules
Computers are essential tools in the modern age, capable of running various programs and multitasking. Here's an
overview of key information:
 ● Computer Rating:
    Computers can run programs that have a rating equal to or lower than their Rating.
 ● Computer Multi-tasking:
    A Computer can run a number of Programs equal to their Rating simultaneously. Interface Rating 0 doesn't count
    against the total number of Programs running in a computer.
   ● Example: A TL9 computer with a Rating of 2 can run two programs simultaneously.
 ● Computer Storage:
    Computers TL9 and above assume unlimited storage. TL8 and 7 can store a number of programs equal to 5 of their
    rating.
 ● Computer Form Factor:
    The Computers by TL assume laptops.

Computer software encompasses various programs that enable users to interact with and utilize computer systems.

● Interface: Also known as the operating system, the Interface is the user's gateway to the computer's functions and
    features. Rating 0 Interface doesn't count against the number of programs that can be run simultaneously.
● Security: Security software focuses on access control and monitoring systems for suspicious activities. It finds and

        46). Rushing the process or extending the analysis time can modify the effectiveness of the software, represented

        through various means, such as passive monitoring devices that collect data from a ship’s computer system,
        malware injections (e.g., from a compromised Jump tape or an unvetted Expert program), or other methods.
   ● Opposed Roll Mechanics: When using Intrusion software against a system, compare its rating to the rating of the

Special Computer Tasks

Denial of Service (DoS)
A Denial of Service attack, commonly referred to as DoS, is an attempt to make a machine, network, or service
unavailable to its intended users. In the context of spacefaring and advanced computer systems, DoS attacks can have
dire consequences, especially when they target critical systems of a ship or station.
● Occasion: A character can initiate a DoS attack using a computer. This can be achieved through specialized software
    like an Agent or Intrusion Software, or by improvising directly from the Interface.
   ● Using Agent/Intrusion Software: The software is designed to efficiently execute the DoS attack, leveraging its
        capabilities to maximize the chances of success.
   ● Improvising from the Interface: This method is less efficient and more prone to errors. When improvising a DoS
        attack:
       ● There's a DM-4 penalty to the roll (DM-2 if using an Intelligent Interface).
       ● The rating of the improvised DoS is equal to the Computer's Rating minus 1.
● Effects: A successful DoS attack prevents the target computer system from processing external inputs or signals for a
    specific task. This can be particularly devastating if, for instance, it prevents a ship from receiving critical navigation
    data or hampers communication systems.
   ● Example: A common application of DoS in space combat is to jam the communications of a target ship, rendering
        it unable to send or receive signals. This can isolate the ship, making it vulnerable to further attacks or
        maneuvers.
● Limitations: A DoS attack can only be executed against a computer system with an equal or lower rating. For
    instance, a TL10 computer with a Rating of 3 cannot launch a DoS attack against a ship's Rating 5 computer. However,
    a TL12 computer could attempt a DoS against a TL7 Rating 5 ship's computer, given the advanced technology and
    capabilities of the higher TL system.
● Mechanics:
   ● Opposed Roll: Executing a DoS is an opposed roll, where both the attacker and the defender roll to determine the
        outcome. The defender must have the ability to React to counter the DoS. Unopposed it becomes an Easy Task
        (TN 6+), otherwise its a Difficult (TN10+) Task.
   ● Rating Advantage: When initiating or defending against a DoS attack, adjust the Target Number (TN) based on the
        difference in computer ratings. If the Attacking system’s rating is higher, decrease the TN by 2 for each rating point
        difference. Conversely, if the defending system’s rating is lower, increase the TN by 2 for each point of difference.
   ● Ship's Computers: Utilizing the ship's primary computers, with ratings of 5, 10, or 15, for a DoS attack is highly
        effective due to their advanced capabilities. These computers occupy about 1DT, distributed throughout the ship,
        ensuring redundancy and efficiency.

Calculating Cost per Seating.
Take the number of Seats by getting the 80% of the Cargo Space, and determining how many Passenger Modules and
their respective seats. Now determine the most likely % of Seats will be Occupied. Divide the Operations per Week by
these minimum seats.

Example:
A 90DT Shuttle has 65DT of cargo. 52 Cramped Passenger Seating modules will fit. That's 260 seats. Lets say that
regulations, competition and seasonal trends puts the minimum seats that will be filled at ~30% or 80 seats. Then the
cost per seat would be 146Cr. If 80 seats are filled the small craft breaks even that week. There can be multiple trips per
week.

In CE SRD page 109 there is an entry of 2,000 Cr per stateroom per month, and for Low berth as well. I recommend
removing it because of the following reasons:
Payment Logistics: How is payment for monthly life support managed when the ship is away from port? So if the ship is
2-3 months away from port, what happens to the usability of the Life support system?
Payment Recipient: To whom is the payment for life support made? If its the ship’s maintenance and engineering team,
aren’t they paid a salary for this already?
Redundancy with Existing Costs: The life support costs seem redundant when considering that players are already
paying for life support supplies and maintenance. What additional expenses does the life support fee cover? If its the
engineering and maintenance of the ship, that's already being given a salary.
Real-world Mission Planning: In real-world space missions, the costs for consumables and supplies are calculated
upfront, along with the requirements for maintaining recycling systems and sealed habitats. What exactly are players
paying for if these costs have already been factored into maintenance and life support?
Crew Cost of Living: Upon analyzing the cost of living for crew members, it becomes evident that a gunner earning 1,000
credits per month can only afford half of the 2,000-credit monthly life support fee. This doesn't even take into account
the daily supply costs of 90 credits. So a Gunner at 1,000cr per month who has to pay his own way pays - 1,000cr to
share a stateroom and 2745 cr for 30 days of life support supplies. The total cost per person is 3,745 cr per sharing a
room on top of the Salaries.
Unnecessary Complexity: The life support fee adds unnecessary overhead for both the Referee and the players. It's more
straightforward to account for the consumption of supplies on a daily basis.
There are no Rules for Cabins and Cockpits and Staying in the Bridge’s life support.

OUTCOME of removing the 2000cr per stateroom per month: Characters spend 90cr per day for life support supplies.
We can assume this is food, air, water, air and water filter replacements, waste disposal and recycling (biologicals to
break things down). Players and Referees will just calculate how many days they were in the ship and calculate that in the
mandays of supplies. Note luxuries ar 120cr per day (CE SRD 109) and will require Luxuries modules CE SRD 130 (high
end food preparing machines, automation that cleans and maintains luxury belongings, and various conveniences and
comforts that can fit 1DT).
